Q1. Which parameters did we optimise? After selecting LightGBM as our final classifier, we experimented with Bayesian optimisation to see whether we could improve its performance further. We searched for the following hyperparameters: num_leaves, learning_rate, n_estimators, min_child_samples, subsample, colsample_bytree and reg_lambda.

Q2. What ranges did we use?

ParameterSearch range
num_leaves15–127 (log)
learning_rate0.01–0.2 (log)
n_estimators100–500, step 50
min_child_samples5–300 (log)
subsample0.5–1
colsample_bytree0.4–1
reg_lambda0.001–10 (log)

Logarithmic sampling was used for parameters where it was appropriate.

Q3. What was the optimisation objective? Our objective was to maximise Top-1 accuracy using 3-fold GroupKFold cross-validation on the training dataset. This allowed us to search for parameters that generalised across different trips.

Q4. How was the optimisation performed? We used Optuna with the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) sampler. We ran 20 trials, using random seed 42 and 6 random starting trials, with a total computation time of approximately 17 minutes. During the process, the machine restarted after trial 14. We recovered the completed trials from the log and reloaded them (scripts/05a_import_trials_from_log.py). An earlier, wider search was also attempted, but we stopped it after 11 trials because it was taking too long.

Q5. What were the best hyperparameters?

ParameterBest value
num_leaves102
learning_rate0.095
n_estimators500
min_child_samples77
subsample0.94
colsample_bytree0.75
reg_lambda1.30

This configuration achieved a 3-fold Top-1 accuracy of 0.8953.

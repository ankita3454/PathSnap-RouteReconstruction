Understanding the problem

The main challenge we are trying to solve is that GPS data is not always accurate. Due to GPS noise, a recorded location may not fall exactly on the road where the vehicle is travelling. Our goal is to identify the correct road segment for every GPS point and use those predictions to reconstruct the actual route.

One difficulty we faced was that the .route file provides the sequence of roads in the correct order, but it does not directly tell us which road corresponds to each GPS point. So, we had to develop our own method to assign labels to individual GPS points, which is explained in Section 2.

How we approached the problem

We treated this as a binary classification problem. For every GPS point, we generated a set of possible road candidates and trained a model to determine whether each candidate was the correct road or not. The model produces probabilities rather than just yes-or-no predictions. We then normalise these probabilities for each GPS point. This is important because the HMM uses these probabilities as emission scores when reconstructing the final route.

Evaluation metrics

Our main evaluation metric was point-level Top-1 accuracy, which measures how often the model selects the correct road as its highest-probability prediction. We also evaluated macro-F1 and log loss. Log loss is particularly important here because the probabilities generated by the classifier are later used by the HMM.

Main results

  • Student-test: the classifier achieved 90.04% accuracy, which improved to 92.51% after applying the HMM. The final route F1 score was 0.96.
  • Evaluator-test: achieved an accuracy of 93.86%.

In fact, some metrics became slightly worse after tuning, particularly log loss and inference time.

Why didn't tuning help? All 20 optimisation trials produced accuracy values between 87.8% and 89.5%. This suggests that the model had reached a performance limit with the available features and classification approach. Most of the remaining errors involved reverse-direction road twins and intersections. These are difficult cases because the classifier evaluates each GPS point independently. Changing its hyperparameters cannot fully solve the problem of ensuring that consecutive road predictions form a realistic route. This is also why introducing the HMM was more effective than simply tuning the classifier further.
